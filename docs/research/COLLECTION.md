# Collection loops — what makes them work (research for the catch layer)

Games studied (from play/knowledge; no private data): Animal Crossing (fish & bugs, museum),
Neko Atsume (cats attracted by what you set out), Pokémon (dex, shinies), Stardew Valley (fish
collection, legendaries), Fishdom / Fishing Clash (as anti-patterns), Meowdoku (cats as reward faces).

## Principles that recur in the ones people play for months
1. **Agency over pure luck.** Neko Atsume's rare cats come from *what you place*; Animal Crossing
   fish depend on where/when you fish. Players chase a rule they can learn. Pure gacha feels empty.
2. **Visible incompleteness.** Silhouettes + a counter ("31 / 48") create a standing goal (the
   Zeigarnik pull). The log must be one tap away but never in the way.
3. **A hint per unknown.** Each locked entry says *how* ("lives in long, narrow pools") so the next
   game has a purpose. Hints turn the collection into soft quests.
4. **First catch is a moment.** A short, distinct celebration the first time; quiet afterwards.
5. **Duplicates still matter.** Size records (Animal Crossing's fish sizes), counts, rare colour
   variants (Pokémon shinies) give a long tail after "complete".
6. **Rarity tiers with a floor.** Commons on day one; rares in week one; legends in weeks. A pity
   timer prevents droughts where nothing new appears for many sessions.
7. **Finite and completable.** ~40–80 entries. Completion should take weeks of normal play, not
   months of grinding or money.
8. **Personality.** One-line flavour text, a name, a pose. The face of the reward (Meowdoku's cats).
9. **Never pay-to-catch.** Fishing Clash-style paid lures destroy trust and the fairness promise.

## Mapping to Lagooner Beach
- Agency → **species are tied to lagoon shapes and conditions.** Our one rule already creates shapes;
  the journal teaches players to build different ones (long thin, square, plus, huge, cascades).
  That turns the collection into a depth tutor for the core mechanic.
- Board stays clean: creatures leap out of the water and dive away; the log lives in the menu.
- Score unchanged (points are the skill measure; catches are the delight layer).
